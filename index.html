A Practical Restaurant Bill Splitting Guide

A good bill split should be easy to explain and should add back to the receipt total. The right method depends on whether everyone ordered similar meals, whether dishes were shared, and how the group wants to distribute tax and gratuity.

Equal split or itemized split?

An equal split works when orders are similar. Divide the final total by the number of diners. An itemized split is better when one person ordered a small meal and another ordered several courses or drinks. A hybrid method assigns personal items individually and divides shared items only among the people who had them.

Build each person's food share

List every personal item first. Then assign shared dishes to the participating diners. A $24 appetizer shared by four people adds $6 to each of their food shares. Someone who did not eat the appetizer should not be included in that division.

Example: Three meals cost $18, $25, and $32. A $15 dessert is shared by the first two diners. Their adjusted food shares are $25.50, $32.50, and $32.00. The adjusted shares still total the $90 food subtotal.

Distribute tax proportionally

When order amounts differ, allocate tax according to each person's percentage of the taxable subtotal. If a diner accounts for 30% of the taxable food, that diner receives 30% of the tax. This method follows the receipt more closely than dividing tax equally.

Check service charges before tipping

Some restaurants add gratuity or a service fee automatically. Read the receipt before adding another tip. If the group chooses a separate tip, decide whether it should be based on the pre-tax food subtotal or another local convention, then apply that rule consistently.

Apply discounts to the right people

A discount on one item belongs to the diner responsible for that item. An order-wide promotion can be allocated proportionally across all eligible food. Record the discounted subtotal before calculating percentage-based tax or tip when the receipt follows that sequence.

Round only once

Keep full precision during the calculation and round each final share to cents. Then add the rounded shares together. If the result differs from the receipt by a few cents, distribute the remainder openly so the group total matches exactly.
