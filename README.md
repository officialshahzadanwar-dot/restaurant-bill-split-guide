# How to Split a Restaurant Bill Fairly

Splitting a restaurant bill becomes confusing when the table includes shared starters, different meal prices, tax, service charges, tips, discounts, or people who did not order the same items. A fair method starts by agreeing on the rule before calculating anything.

## Choose the split method

Use an equal split when everyone ordered roughly the same amount and the group prefers speed. Divide the final total by the number of diners.

Use an itemized split when individual orders differ significantly. Assign personal items to the diner who ordered them, divide shared dishes among the people who ate them, and then distribute tax and tip consistently.

For mixed groups, a hybrid method is often best: personal meals stay individual, shared food is divided among participants, and common charges are allocated proportionally.

## Start from the subtotal

Separate the receipt into four parts:

1. Personal items
2. Shared items
3. Tax or service charges
4. Tip

Suppose four people order personal meals costing $18, $24, $20, and $28. They also share a $16 appetizer. The food subtotal is $106.

If everyone shares the appetizer equally, add $4 to each personal food total. Their adjusted food amounts become $22, $28, $24, and $32.

## Allocate tax and tip

An equal allocation is simple, but it can be unfair when orders are very different. A proportional allocation assigns tax and tip according to each person's share of the food subtotal.

```text
person's proportion = person's adjusted food amount / food subtotal
person's tax = total tax × person's proportion
person's tip = total tip × person's proportion
```

If one diner represents 25% of the food subtotal, that diner receives 25% of the tax and tip.

When a restaurant has already added a service charge, confirm whether the group intends to add another tip. Do not accidentally tip twice.

## Handle discounts correctly

Apply an order-wide discount proportionally unless the offer belongs to a specific item. For a 10% discount on the whole order, reduce every diner's pre-tax share by 10%. For a free dessert ordered by one person, apply the discount only to that person.

Gift cards and cash payments are payment methods, not discounts. Calculate what each person owes first, then record how each share was paid.

## Check the total and rounding

Round individual shares to cents only at the end. Early rounding can create a small difference between the sum of the shares and the receipt total. If a few cents remain, assign them transparently to one or more diners so the final shares reconcile exactly.

## Final checklist

- Confirm who shared each item.
- Check whether tax is already included.
- Check whether a service charge or gratuity is already included.
- Apply discounts before allocating tax and tip when appropriate.
- Round at the end.
- Make sure all individual shares add back to the exact restaurant total.

A fair split does not have to be complicated. It needs a clear rule, consistent allocation, and one final reconciliation check.
